Web research pass, 2026-08-13, ahead of Review 1. Pure web-search/web-fetch survey of the current BCI and BBI landscape, done to (a) check `paper2/main.tex`'s BBI throughput retrodiction against anything published since the `literature/bbi_throughput_survey.md` pass (2026-07-31), and (b) look for anyone in the field independently arguing that bandwidth/channel capacity is *not* the real bottleneck — which would bear directly on the paper's "convention bottleneck" claim, either as support or as a novelty threat.

**A note on source quality up front, because it matters for how much weight to put on some of the numbers below:** a general web search on "Neuralink 2026," "BCI 2026," etc. surfaces a lot of low-quality aggregator/content-farm sites (`technology.org`, `techbuzzai.com`, `3zebras.com`, `beyondtmrw.org`, `tech4impactsummit.com`, `neurapod.com`, `teslanorth.com`, `pdpspectra.com`) that restate press-release numbers without bylines or citations, and in at least one case (a claimed "120 words per minute" for Neuralink's second patient) I could not corroborate the figure against any primary source. I've flagged every number below by source tier: **primary** (company blog, peer-reviewed paper, clinicaltrials.gov), **journalistic** (named byline, editorial outlet), or **aggregator** (unbylined SEO content — treat with real skepticism).

## 1. Recent BCI advances, 2023–2026

### Academic speech/motor decoding — the actual state of the art

- **Willett, F.R. et al. (2023). "A high-performance speech neuroprosthesis." *Nature* 620, 1031–1036.** [nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06377-x](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06377-x) — Stanford/BrainGate, participant with ALS, intracortical microelectrode arrays. Decoded speech at **62 words/minute** (vs. a prior handwriting-BCI record of ~90 characters/min from the same group's 2021 paper), 9.1% word error rate on a 50-word vocabulary, 23.8% WER on a 125,000-word vocabulary, phoneme classification at 62% accuracy over 39 phonemes. **Directly relevant**: this is the current best-documented academic ceiling for invasive speech BCI throughput, useful as the "state of the art" anchor for point 1.
- **Metzger, S.L. et al. (2023). "A high-performance neuroprosthesis for speech decoding and avatar control." *Nature* 620, 1037–1046.** [nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06443-4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37612505/) — UCSF/Chang lab, participant with brainstem-stroke anarthria, 253-channel ECoG. **78 words/minute median**, 25% median WER; simultaneously decoded to text, synthesized speech audio, and drove a facial avatar. **Relevant**: current highest wpm figure I found in the literature for any speech-BCI system; still well under natural speech (150–200 wpm, which the Chang lab itself cites as the target).
- **Metzger, S.L. et al. (2025). "A streaming brain-to-voice neuroprosthesis to restore naturalistic communication." *Nature Neuroscience.*** [nature.com/articles/s41593-025-01905-6](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-025-01905-6) — follow-up work, moves from post-hoc decoding to real-time streaming voice synthesis with the same participant population. **Relevant**: shows the 2023 result is being consolidated into lower-latency real-time systems, not superseded by a step-change in throughput.
- **Card, N.S. et al. (2024). "An Accurate and Rapidly Calibrating Speech Neuroprosthesis." *NEJM* 391:7.** [nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2314132](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2314132) — BrainGate consortium (Stanford/Brown/Mass General/VA), participant with ALS, focuses on fast calibration (usable within minutes) rather than raw speed; comparable WER territory to Willett 2023. **Relevant**: shows the frontier moving toward usability/calibration speed rather than raw bits/second — consistent with the idea that raw channel throughput is not the axis the field itself treats as most bottlenecked right now.

### Historical point for comparison — pre-2020 intracortical cursor-control baseline

Multiple point-and-click intracortical BCI studies from the mid-2010s (Willett et al. 2017, *J. Neural Eng.* 14:026010, "Signal-independent noise in intracortical brain-computer interfaces causes movement time properties inconsistent with Fitts' law," [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28177925](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28177925); and related work summarized in search results) reported **peak ~3.16 bits/second, average ~2.90 bits/second** for cursor control. This is the baseline against which Neuralink's 2024–2025 claims (below) should be read.

### Industry — Neuralink

- **Primary source**: [neuralink.com/updates/](https://neuralink.com/updates/) (PRIME Study Progress Update, A Year of Telepathy, Two Years of Telepathy, GB-PRIME Study Launch, CONVOY Study pages — these are JS-rendered and I could not extract body text via WebFetch, so figures below come from journalistic/secondary corroboration of Neuralink's own public claims, not directly from the blog HTML).
- **Regalado, A. (2025-01-16). "What to expect from Neuralink in 2025." *MIT Technology Review*.** [technologyreview.com/2025/01/16/1110017](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/01/16/1110017/what-to-expect-from-neuralink-in-2025/) — journalistic, named byline. Reports Neuralink's own claim that first patient Noland Arbaugh achieved **"over nine bits per second,"** which Neuralink says **"doubled the old brain-interface record"** (i.e., roughly the ~2.9–3.16 bps academic baseline above). Able-bodied users score ~10 bps on Neuralink's own WebGrid benchmark. Also reports a real hardware failure mode: **more than half of Arbaugh's electrode threads retracted**, degrading control and requiring up to 45-minute recalibration sessions. 3 patients as of Jan 2025.
- Multiple aggregator sources (not independently verified against a primary Neuralink post) put the participant count at **21 by early 2026**, zero serious device-related adverse events, the N2 implant carrying **4,096 electrodes vs. 1,024 in N1** (a 4x increase), and a "VOICE" trial targeting speech restoration at a stated goal of 140 wpm. Treat the 4x electrode-count figure as plausible (consistent with Neuralink's known roadmap) but not confirmed from a primary source in this pass.
- **The pattern worth flagging for the paper**: Neuralink's own claimed number (~9–10.5 bps cursor control) sits right at the pre-existing ~10 bps "able-bodied mouse user" ceiling — a ceiling that long predates Neuralink and is not a hardware artifact of any particular BCI system. Going from 1,024 to 4,096 electrodes did not push throughput past that ceiling; it pushed the *academic* 2.9–3.16 bps baseline up to roughly double, converging on — not exceeding — a pre-existing behavioral ceiling. This is a smaller-scale, mainstream-BCI echo of the same pattern the paper's BBI retrodiction describes: more hardware, throughput converging on some other limit rather than scaling with channel count.

### Industry — Synchron, Precision Neuroscience, Paradromics

- **Synchron** — Stentrode, delivered via blood vessel (no open-brain surgery). May 2025: first native integration with **Apple's BCI Human Interface Device (BCI HID) protocol**, registering the implant with iOS/iPadOS as a new input class rather than emulating mouse/touch, feeding into Apple's existing Switch Control accessibility stack. August 2025: public demo of ALS patient Mark Jackson navigating an iPad, composing text, and opening apps by thought alone. 10 patients implanted; pivotal trial targeted for 2026; $200M Series D raised Nov 2025. [Sources: massdevice.com, mobihealthnews.com, businesswire.com — journalistic/trade press, converging accounts]. **Relevant**: notable because it reframes the interface problem as a *protocol/software* integration problem (a new HID class, riding on an existing accessibility stack) rather than a raw-bandwidth problem — Synchron's own lower-bandwidth vascular approach is explicitly positioned as sufficient for this class of task.
- **Precision Neuroscience** — Layer 7 Cortical Interface, thin-film surface electrode array. Received **FDA 510(k) clearance in 2025** for up to 30-day implantation; published in *Nature Biomedical Engineering* on high-resolution recording/stimulation without cortical damage. [Source: aggregator summary of researchgate-hosted comparative review, not independently verified against Precision's own site in this pass — flagged as a gap.]
- **Paradromics** — Connexus device. **June 2025**: first-in-human recording, University of Michigan epilepsy-surgery patient, temporary implant, removed intact in under 20 minutes, described as first-in-human for the device. [Source: aggregator/trade summaries — same caveat.]

### Gap to flag honestly

I could not find, in this pass, a rigorous apples-to-apples bits/second comparison across Neuralink/Synchron/Precision/Paradromics — the companies report different metrics (cursor bps, characters/min, "first successful recording") that resist direct comparison. Anyone wanting a clean SOTA number for "2026 BCI throughput" would need to go to each company's own clinical-trial data releases or wait for peer-reviewed writeups; press coverage alone doesn't standardize the metric.

## 2. Brain-to-brain interfaces (BBI) specifically, since Jiang et al. 2019

This section updates `literature/bbi_throughput_survey.md` (2026-07-31), which already established the Rao 2014 → Jiang 2019 comparison and found no post-2019 human BBI study with a reported throughput figure via a general search covering 2021–2024. This pass extended the search window through 2026 and added an animal-BBI check; **the result is unchanged: no new direct human-to-human or animal-to-animal BBI study with a quantified information-transfer figure surfaced.**

- **Vakilipour, P. et al. (2024). "Brain‐to‐brain interface technology: A brief history, current state, and future goals." *International Journal of Developmental Neuroscience* 84(5), 351–367.** [onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jdn.10334](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jdn.10334); PubMed: [38711277](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38711277/). **Already in this repo's bibliography** (`vakilipour2024brain`), so this isn't a new find for Ashok — flagging it here for completeness. I was unable to extract the full abstract via WebFetch (paywalled/cookie-gated), so treat the content note below as a search-result composite, not a verified quote: the review covers BBI's history from the 2013 Rao/Stocco EEG-TMS work through BrainNet, catalogs rehab/ALS/locked-in-syndrome applications, and discusses ethics and future goals. Given it's already cited in the paper's bibliography, Ashok's team has presumably already extracted whatever it says about bottlenecks — worth double-checking whether it independently frames the field's limit in hardware terms or otherwise, since the search-result composite didn't surface that detail cleanly.
- **No new anchor data point.** A direct search for 2021–2026 human BBI studies (EEG+TMS or otherwise) returned only restatements of Rao 2014 and Jiang 2019, plus unrelated TMS-EEG methodology papers (about using TMS-EEG to probe cortical circuits generally, not to link two brains). A separate search for animal BBI/"Brainet" work post-2015 (the Duke Nicolelis-lab rat/monkey "Brainets") also returned nothing newer than the original 2015 studies.
- **Absence-of-evidence caveat, repeated from the prior survey and still applicable**: general web search is not a substitute for a Scopus/Web-of-Science systematic search. It is possible a niche 2022–2026 BBI paper exists that doesn't surface in general search. But two independent search passes five months apart, using different query strategies, both came up empty — this raises confidence that the plateau-through-2026 claim is at least not contradicted by anything easily discoverable, which is the most this kind of search can establish.

**Bottom line for point 2**: the paper's retrodiction stands unchallenged by this pass. If anything, the *complete absence* of any new BBI throughput data point in 7+ years (2019–2026) while adjacent BCI throughput has visibly moved (Section 1) makes the divergence between BCI progress and BBI stagnation more, not less, striking.

## 3. Key labs, groups, and where the field says the bottleneck is

**Active labs/groups surfaced in this pass**: Stanford BrainGate (Willett, Henderson, Shenoy legacy, Druckmann), UCSF Chang lab (Metzger, Chang), Brown University BrainGate site, Caltech Andersen lab (parietal-cortex BCI, also home to the Zheng/Meister "10 bits/s" work), University of Washington (Rao/Stocco — the original 2014/2019 BBI authors), University of Michigan (Paradromics's first-in-human partner). Industry: Neuralink, Synchron, Precision Neuroscience, Paradromics.

**Where the field's own voices put the bottleneck** — this splits into two camps that are worth naming explicitly:

**Camp A — bandwidth/channel capacity is still the thing to push.** Paradromics's own blog explicitly argues this position: [paradromics.com/blog/brain-speed-bci-potential](https://www.paradromics.com/blog/brain-speed-bci-potential) (Feb 2026) pushes back on the "10 bits/s is all you need" framing (see Camp B below), arguing that while *conscious decision output* may be ~10 bps, rich embodied experience requires much more (cites speech ~40 bps, joint/motor coordination ~465 bps), and that task design — restoring mere function vs. preserving embodied richness — determines whether high hardware bandwidth matters. This is, unsurprisingly, also a company whose product differentiator is a high-channel-count intracortical array, so the position is not disinterested, but it's a real industry counter-voice worth having on record.

**Camp B — the bottleneck is downstream of hardware.** This is the more surprising and more paper-relevant finding — see Section 4.

## 4. Work suggesting bandwidth is NOT the constraint — the most important section

This is the section most relevant to the paper's specific claim (BBI throughput is capped by how much shared "convention"/code can be pre-installed, not by hardware/channel capacity). Three independent 2024–2026 sources converge on "it's not raw bandwidth," from three different angles. None of them use the paper's specific "convention" / observational-vs-interventional-identifiability framing — that framing appears to still be original — but all three would read as corroborating the paper's general intuition that more channels ≠ more meaningful throughput, and are worth citing as independent support.

- **Zheng, J. & Meister, M. (2024/2025). "The unbearable slowness of being: Why do we live at 10 bits/s?" *Neuron*, DOI 10.1016/j.neuron.2024.11.008. Preprint: arXiv:2408.10234.** [arxiv.org/abs/2408.10234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10234) | [Caltech coverage](https://www.caltech.edu/about/news/thinking-slowly-the-paradoxical-slowness-of-human-behavior). Caltech (Meister lab). **Core finding**: human sensory systems take in information at **~10⁹ bits/s**, but human behavioral *output* — measured across reading, typing, video games, Rubik's-cube solving — tops out at **~10 bits/s**, a nine-order-of-magnitude gap. They propose a two-tier brain architecture (a fast, high-dimensional "outer brain" and a slow, low-dimensional "inner brain" that gates behavior) to explain the gap. The abstract itself doesn't discuss BCI/BBI directly, but the number is exactly the ceiling that Neuralink's own cursor-control results (Section 1) appear to be converging toward. **Relevance**: strong independent support for "the constraint isn't the channel, it's something on the output/decision side" — though framed as a *general* neuroscience finding about behavior, not specifically about coupling between two systems or about convention/protocol.
- **Meyer, L.M. & Zamani, M. (2026). "But do we need high bandwidth? Applications and scaling challenges of invasive brain–computer interfaces." *Journal of Neural Engineering* 23(3), 031001.** [iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1741-2552/ae6dfd](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1741-2552/ae6dfd). **Core finding**: recording bandwidth has doubled roughly every 7 years, but this hasn't translated proportionally into clinical benefit. They identify the real constraint as an **"input/output disparity"** — megabits of raw neural data collapse to only bits of meaningful behavioral output, because neural populations occupy low-dimensional manifolds regardless of recording scale, and speech/motor decoding still leans on external priors (language models) to close the gap rather than raw signal fidelity. Explicit quote (as fetched): *"sophisticated decoding alone cannot compensate for limited signal fidelity"* and the bottleneck is "not channel count but the structure of tasks, user cognition, and the need for assistive intelligence." **Relevance**: this is close to a direct statement, from inside mainstream BCI engineering (not BBI, not the paper's framework), that hardware/channel capacity is not the limiting factor — one of the closer independent echoes of the paper's stance I found. Peer-reviewed venue, which matters for weight.
- **Jiang, B. (2026). "More Electrodes, Faster Minds? Rethinking Bandwidth in Brain–Computer Interfaces." arXiv:2607.24820 [q-bio.NC], submitted 2026-07-17.** [arxiv.org/abs/2607.24820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24820). **Core finding**: distinguishes physical interface bandwidth, decodable neural information, and "confirmed subject-level I/O" as three different quantities that don't scale together. Argues higher-capacity interfaces yield real but *diminishing* and *nonlinear* gains, because meaningful human I/O is constrained by "embodiment, learning, and subject expression" — e.g., user selection/confirmation/authorization gate what neural information actually becomes communication, and skills can't be "uploaded" instantly but require calibration across "muscle control, proprioception, body dynamics, and the environment." **Relevance and caveat**: this is the single closest hit to the paper's thesis I found in this pass — it's explicitly about *why electrode count doesn't linearly buy meaningful throughput*, which is adjacent to the paper's claim that BBI throughput is capped by convention rather than channel capacity. But treat it cautiously: it's a **single-author, non-peer-reviewed arXiv preprint posted less than a month before this scan (2026-07-17)**, with no venue, and I could not verify the author's affiliation or track record. It should be read as "someone else is independently circling the same intuition, very recently, in a raw preprint" — not as an established, peer-reviewed anticipation of the paper's claim. Worth monitoring for whether it gets picked up/cited, and worth reading in full before deciding whether it needs a footnote in `main.tex`.

**On the novelty question specifically**: none of these three papers frame their finding in terms of what the paper's core theoretical claim needs — that coupling/understanding is not *identifiable from observational data alone*, only from intervention, and that what looks like a hardware ceiling in BBI is actually a ceiling on how much shared convention/protocol can be established or pre-installed between two systems. Zheng & Meister and Meyer & Zamani are about single-brain behavioral output and single-system BCI decoding, not about the dyadic coupling/convention problem at all. Jiang's arXiv preprint gets closer (it's explicitly about the gap between decodable information and *confirmed, expressed* communication, which has a whiff of the observational/interventional distinction) but still doesn't name convention, shared code, or identifiability as the mechanism. **My read: the paper's specific theoretical contribution — that the bottleneck is identifiability of coupling from observation vs. intervention, cashed out as a pre-installed-convention constraint — remains undescribed in the current literature as of this scan.** The general "bandwidth isn't really the bottleneck" intuition, however, now has three independent, recent, on-topic sources behind it, up from what the repo had before this pass. That's useful ammunition for the paper's framing even though it isn't a scoop threat.
