Web research pass, 2026-08-13, for paper2 ("Understanding Is Not Observable"). Covers the fourth of the paper's four target fields — comparative cognition / animal communication — following prior passes on AI, neuroscience (hyperscanning), and philosophy. Scope per brief: (1) current methods for testing animal "understanding," (2) Clever Hans as historical and live methodological concern, (3) whether ablation/intervention controls are already standard practice, (4) any 2023-2026 work approaching a formal non-identifiability argument for animal comprehension specifically.

**Headline finding, up front:** comparative cognition is very plausibly the field furthest along the paper's own prescription. Playback experiments (present/withhold/scramble the signal, measure the behavioral consequence) are the field's standard tool and have been for decades; "Clever Hans effect" is invoked by name as a live, standing threat, including in 2025 papers explicitly bridging comparative cognition to AI/LLM evaluation. No source found states the identifiability argument in this paper's form (a formal proof that observational statistics cannot separate genuine comprehension from conditioned response), but the field's own methodological self-critique (Buckner 2017; SEP's "Underdetermination" section) circles the same problem under the label "underdetermination," and resolves it by appeal to epistemic values and neural mechanism rather than by a general identifiability argument. This is a near-miss worth citing, not a scoop that undercuts novelty.

## 1. How comparative cognition currently tests "understanding"

**Functional reference (vervet alarm calls) — status in 2023-2025.** The founding paradigm (Seyfarth, Cheney & Marler 1980) holds that vervet monkey alarm calls are "functionally referential" if they are (i) stimulus-specific and (ii) context-independent — i.e., elicit the right escape response without needing contextual cues (Marler, Evans & Hauser 1992; criteria via Macedonia & Evans 1993). Both criteria have been under sustained attack since ~2009-2012 and the attack has hardened in recent years:
- **Wheeler & Fischer (2012)**, "Functionally referential signals: a promising paradigm whose time has passed," *Evolutionary Anthropology* 21:195-205. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/evan.21319 — Argues functional reference is at best marginal: most animal signals are neither stimulus-specific nor context-independent (animals routinely use contextual cues to interpret calls), and even vervet alarm calls fail stimulus-specificity once you look only at male calls (citing Price et al. 2015). Also argues the calls are typically not voluntarily produced, undermining the "precursor of words" framing. Proposes abandoning production-side functional reference for a receiver-centred approach. *Relevance: direct precedent for treating the classic paradigm's evidentiary basis as broken, on very similar "the observed correlation doesn't licence the semantic conclusion" grounds — though framed as an empirical/definitional dispute, not a formal identifiability claim.*
- **Deshpande, van de Waal & Zuberbühler (2023)**, "Context-dependent alarm responses in wild vervet monkeys," *Animal Cognition*, PMC10345060. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10345060/ — Male vervet "bark" alarm calls are given both for terrestrial predators and during intergroup encounters; playback of the same bark elicited stronger anti-predator responses when it was NOT accompanied by intergroup-encounter cues than when it was, showing receivers use causal/contextual inference to disambiguate an acoustically identical signal rather than mapping call-type to referent directly. *Relevance: shows the field already treats "same signal, different response depending on context" as diagnostic — i.e., they know raw call-type correlation with behavior is not sufficient evidence of what the receiver has inferred.*
- **Berthet, Mesbahi, Pajot, Cäsar, Neumann & Zuberbühler (2019)**, "Titi monkeys combine alarm calls to create probabilistic meaning," *Science Advances* 5(5):eaav3991. https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aav3991 — Titi monkeys encode predator type/location via the *proportion* of "B-calls" in a sequence (probabilistic, not categorical, encoding). Established the encoding side by field recording, then confirmed the decoding side with **playback experiments** manipulating call-sequence composition and measuring listener response — the ablation/intervention move applied directly to the semantic-content question. *Relevance: a clean instance of the field pairing an observational (encoding) claim with an interventional (decoding/playback) claim before asserting "comprehension" — exactly the two-rung structure opp.md argues for.*
- **Sooty mangabey and magpie work (2023-2024)** extend the same "acoustic structure + context-dependent comprehension" program to other species (e.g., comprehension of own- vs. other-species alarm calls in mangabeys, *Behav. Ecol. Sociobiol.* 2023; functionally referential signaling in azure-winged magpies, 2024) — the paradigm remains active and contested but is not abandoned.

**Ape language studies (Kanzi, Nim, Washoe) — legacy and 2025-2026 developments.**
- **SEP, "Animal Communication"** (Richard Moore & Giulia Palazzolo, first published 2024-10-23). https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/animal-communication/ — Section 2.2 is the best current synthesis of the "do animals know word meanings" debate. Key passage: Terrace (director of the Nim project) worried that "agents who lack concepts could nonetheless use words to manipulate others, without understanding them, in the same way that one might manipulate the keys on a keyboard to guide a character in a computer game" — his proposed fix is to look for *spontaneous, non-reward-tied information sharing* rather than request behavior, since request behavior is directly reinforced and therefore uninformative about comprehension. Section 3.3 covers Kanzi's syntax comprehension: he tracks word order ("put the tomato in the oil" vs. "put oil in the tomato") but fails hierarchical/non-linear binding ("fetch the tomato and the oil"), suggesting linear decoding rather than structural parsing (Truswell 2017). *Relevance: Terrace's "manipulating keys on a keyboard" framing is, almost verbatim, the apparent-vs-functional-coupling gap this paper formalizes — a system whose external behavior is indistinguishable between "understands" and "learned to produce the reinforced output." The field's fix (look for unrewarded spontaneous behavior) is a proxy for intervention but is not itself an ablation/randomization design — it is a different observational signature, still rung-one.*
- **Bastos & Krupenye (2026)**, "Evidence for representation of pretend objects by Kanzi, a language-trained bonobo," *Science*, Feb 2026, DOI 10.1126/science.adz0743. https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adz0743 — Kanzi (tested shortly before his March 2025 death) correctly located a "pretend" object (e.g., imaginary juice) across trials at above-chance rates (~78% and ~69% in two conditions) in a scaffolded pretense paradigm modeled on 1980s child-development studies. Note: single-subject study; the SEP entry and other commentary caution against strong generalization from an n=1, decades-enculturated animal. *Relevance: freshest (2026) data point in the "what counts as ape comprehension" literature; illustrates the field is still generating single-subject, request/prompt-driven evidence of the classic type, not ablation-style evidence.*
- Historical grounding: Terrace's Project Nim (1979) explicitly diagnosed Nim's "sentences" as trainer-cued rather than linguistic — see §2 below.

**Waggle dance decoding.** The "dance language controversy" (von Frisch's claim that bees decode a symbolic vector from the waggle dance, vs. Wenner & Wells's argument that odor cues alone suffice) was resolved decisively in von Frisch's favor via a genuinely interventional method: **robotic/mechanical bee experiments** (first built by Michelsen et al. 1989-1992; extended with computer-controlled dancing robots since) that broadcast a *pure*, manipulable waggle signal stripped of a real bee's odor and let researchers **independently vary the encoded distance/direction and check whether recruits go to the manipulated, not the true, location.** Recruits followed the manipulated signal, confirming the dance itself (not co-located odor) carries the spatial information. Recent work (2023-2025, e.g. a 2025 *Behav. Ecol. Sociobiol.* paper on encoding/decoding, and PNAS Nexus 2023 on "waggle drift") continues to refine *how much* variance is signal vs. noise, but the core "decode vs. don't" question is treated as settled by the robot-bee intervention. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02838860 (historical review); https://academic.oup.com/pnasnexus/article/2/9/pgad275/7251052 (2023 ML re-analysis). *Relevance: the single cleanest historical example in comparative cognition of exactly the paper's prescribed remedy — build/ablate the channel, not just observe it — resolving a decades-long observational stalemate.*

## 2. Clever Hans: historical case and live methodological concern

**The original case.** Clever Hans, a horse exhibited c. 1900-1904 as able to do arithmetic, was shown by psychologist Oskar Pfungst (1907) to be reading unconscious postural/facial cues from his questioner (a tensing that relaxed once the correct hoof-tap count was reached). Pfungst's diagnostic method was itself interventional: he **varied whether the questioner knew the answer**, **varied whether Hans could see the questioner** (blinders), and **used unfamiliar questioners** — i.e., he ablated the candidate cue channels one at a time until the "arithmetic" ability vanished. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clever_Hans

**As a live methodological concern, 2024-2025:**
- **Voudouris, Cheke & Schulz (2025)**, "Bringing Comparative Cognition To Computers," arXiv:2503.02882. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.02882 — Explicitly names Clever Hans "a cornerstone of comparative research," used to organize a taxonomy of under- vs. over-attribution errors, and directly maps it onto AI: adversarial-example brittleness in deep vision networks is framed as "AI's own Clever Hans moments" — models that "excel at identifying common objects" but collapse under imperceptible perturbations, revealing reliance on superficial (texture) cues rather than the represented category. Quote: *"Without scrutinising these tests to determine if a Clever Hans effect could be at play, we risk overstating how similar AI systems are to humans and other animals."* This paper is itself a direct instance of the paper's claimed cross-field bridge (comparative cognition → AI evaluation), independently made in 2025. *High relevance: cite both for the Clever Hans framing and as evidence the AI/comparative-cognition bridge this paper draws is already being built by others, from the comparative-cognition side.*
- **Rane, Kirkman, Todd, Royka, Law, Cartmill & Foster (2025)**, "Position: Principles of Animal Cognition to Improve LLM Evaluations," *ICML 2025* (PMLR 267:82051-82061), Oral. https://openreview.net/forum?id=gCPJFcHskT — Proposes five principles from animal-cognition methodology (full text not directly retrievable — OpenReview PDF blocked from this environment; details from abstract and secondary listings) for evaluating LLMs, grounded in a transitive-inference case study. A companion paper, "Principles of Animal Cognition for LLM Evaluations: A Case Study on Transitive Inference" (openreview id RSbSX0Bf24), elaborates the empirical case. *Relevance: second independent 2025 instance of comparative-cognition methodologists explicitly importing their field's tools into AI evaluation — reinforces that this cross-field connection is contemporaneously recognized, which the paper should acknowledge rather than claim as novel connective tissue.*
- **Shapira et al. (2024)**, "Clever Hans or Neural Theory of Mind? Stress Testing Social Reasoning in Large Language Models," *EACL 2024*. https://aclanthology.org/2024.eacl-long.138/ — Uses "Clever Hans" explicitly as the diagnostic label for LLM theory-of-mind benchmark performance that evaporates under prompt perturbation/rephrasing — the same shortcut-learning concern, applied to LLMs rather than animals. (Likely already surfaced by the prior AI-focused research pass; flagged here because it's the direct terminological bridge between the two literatures.)
- **Frontiers work on humanoid robots (2023-2024, exact citation not fully pinned down in this pass)** — proposes robots as a tool to isolate/control unconscious human cueing channels in animal-emotion and cognition research, i.e., a modern engineering solution to the same problem Pfungst solved by hand in 1907.
- **Dog cognition, direct empirical test:** **Schmidjell, Range, Huber & Virányi (2012)**, "Do Owners Have a Clever Hans Effect on Dogs? Results of a Pointing Study," *Frontiers in Psychology* 3:558, PMC3530188. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3530188/ — Directly tests for Clever Hans cueing in the dog-pointing paradigm: found no evidence of unintentional owner cueing when owners were not instructed to cue (dogs chose randomly absent a real point, even when owners knew the food location), but confirmed owners *could* successfully cue dogs when deliberately instructed to. Establishes both that the effect is a genuine risk and that it is empirically testable/rulable-out by design, not just assumed. *Direct, title-level instance of "Clever Hans" as an active, named, tested methodological hypothesis in 2012-era work still cited as the reference test today.*
- **ManyDogs Project (2023)**, "ManyDogs 1: A Multi-Lab Replication Study of Dogs' Pointing Comprehension," *Animal Behavior and Cognition* 10(3):232-286. https://www.animalbehaviorandcognition.org/uploads/journals/53/3%20ManyDogs_ABC_10(3).pdf — Large multi-lab, pre-registered replication asking whether dogs treat human pointing as a social-communicative gesture (comprehension) or a low-level associative cue — the comprehension-vs-conditioned-response question directly, at consortium scale, with open-science/replication safeguards against exactly the kind of unreplicated single-lab claim that produced past Clever-Hans-style overclaims. *Relevance: shows the "associative vs. genuine" distinction is treated today as an empirical question worth a large coordinated multi-lab effort, not dismissed as unanswerable.*

**Does anyone frame the fix as ablation/randomization rather than mere blinding?** Yes, but implicitly rather than as an articulated general principle. Pfungst's own resolution of the original case *was* a form of ablation (removing visual access to the cue, removing the cue-giver's own knowledge of the answer). Playback experiments (below) are explicit signal-ablation/substitution designs. But no source found explicitly generalizes "ablate the cue" into a stated methodological rule the way opp.md does ("functional coupling is what survives removal of the signal") — the field's explicit vocabulary is "double-blind procedures and randomization," with ablation-style playback treated as a separate, field-specific tool (used heavily in bioacoustics/vocal communication) rather than unified with the double-blind/cueing-control literature under one banner.

## 3. Is ablation/intervention already standard practice?

**Yes, and it is closer to standard than in any of the paper's other three fields.** Two independent lines of evidence converge:

**(a) Playback experiments are the field's default tool for exactly this question.** From a methods-overview search: *"Playback is the technique of rebroadcasting natural or synthetic signals to animals and observing their response. The ability to present a putative signal in isolation, without the potential confounding effects of other activities of the signaller, is the main reason for the depth and range of our knowledge of communication systems."* (McGregor, *Playback and Studies of Animal Communication*, Springer — a full monograph on the methodology; see also "Playback experiments: design and analysis" methods paper.) This is architecturally identical to opp.md's prescribed remedy: **isolate the channel, manipulate/withhold/scramble what passes through it, measure the behavioral consequence** — as opposed to observing the signal and the response together and inferring a link. The Berthet et al. titi-monkey study and Deshpande et al. vervet study above are both instances of exactly this design applied to the semantic-comprehension question specifically (not just "does the animal react to sound" but "does the animal decode this particular content").

**(b) Double-blind/cue-control designs targeting Clever Hans specifically are decades-old standard practice**, not a recent addition — see Schmidjell et al. (2012) and its lineage above, and the ManyDogs consortium's institutionalization of pre-registration partly for this reason. The SEP "Methods in Comparative Cognition" entry (Halina/unnamed author[s], first published 2023-09-06 — full text at https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/comparative-cognition/) documents this as baked into experimental design: *"solving the task requires some basic functions... beyond these basic functions, the cognitive skill or capacity under investigation is required to perform successfully on the task; and all other confounding factors are eliminated or controlled for."* Its §3.5 "Reproducibility" section also documents the field's active (2018-2024) confrontation with its own replication crisis, pre-registration push, and multi-lab "ManyPrimates/ManyBirds/ManyDogs" consortia — machinery aimed at exactly the kind of unreplicated single-cue overclaim Clever Hans exemplifies.

**Caveat — this is not identical to the paper's ablation prescription.** The field's default control removes *confounding cues* (visual contact with a trainer, odor trails, contextual information) to isolate *which* channel carries information. It is less commonly deployed as a test of whether *a receiver's downstream behavior causally depends on the content of an already-identified channel* in the specific "ablate a channel known to carry the signal and see if outcome-relevant behavior changes" sense opp.md means (its BBI/receiver-hidden-layer thought experiment). The titi-monkey and vervet-bark playback studies above are the closest matches to this exact design; they are not universal across the field's comprehension claims (e.g., much of the Kanzi/great-ape literature relies on live-interaction correctness rates, not systematic withholding/scrambling of the cue channel).

**Net assessment for the paper:** this is good news to cite explicitly, framed carefully — comparative cognition is the field with the most mature intervention culture of the paper's four, and its own historical resolution of the waggle-dance controversy (robot bee) and its treatment of Clever Hans (Pfungst's original cue-ablation diagnosis, replicated methodologically in dog-pointing studies) are close, non-trivial precedents for the paper's central methodological claim. The paper can legitimately say: "one of the four fields already enforces something close to this discipline, and its most durable resolved controversies (waggle dance) were resolved exactly this way" — which strengthens rather than threatens the argument, since it shows the remedy is not exotic, just under-applied elsewhere (AI eval, hyperscanning).

## 4. Any 2023-2026 near-miss to a formal non-identifiability argument?

**No source found states the general claim in the paper's form** — a proof, or even an explicit formal argument, that observational statistics *cannot in principle* distinguish "genuine comprehension" from "appropriately-conditioned response" for animal signal-receivers, analogous to opp.md's identifiability claim. That specific formalization does not appear to exist in the comparative cognition or philosophy-of-animal-cognition literature as of this scan. This is consistent with (not contradicted by) everything else found below and is worth stating plainly as a gap the paper can legitimately claim.

**However, several sources circle the same problem closely enough to require citation and careful differentiation:**

- **Buckner (2017)**, "Understanding Associative and Cognitive Explanations in Comparative Psychology," in *The Routledge Handbook of Philosophy of Animal Minds* (accepted manuscript at https://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/16335/). Buckner argues the field's "Standard Practice" (devise a task that rules out associative learning, thereby proving cognition) is caught in an **unwinnable arms race**: *"A clever cognitivist will devise an experimental test that cannot be passed using current principles of associative learning theory... A clever associationist will then devise a modest extension of prior associative learning theory that can allow associative models to pass the cognitivist's benchmark... For many different faculties, this back-and-forth appears capable of continuing indefinitely."* His conclusion is that if associative and cognitive explanations are treated as mutually exclusive, "the hard-nosed associationists will probably win the field — and cognition does not exist" from pure behavioral evidence; his proposed fix is to **abandon behavioral underdetermination as the arbiter and appeal to neural mechanism** (distinct memory systems) instead. *This is the closest thing found to an in-principle argument that behavioral/observational data alone cannot settle the comprehension question — but it is argued informally (via an inductive "arms race never terminates" pattern, not a formal non-identifiability proof), and its proposed remedy is neuroscientific decomposition, not causal intervention/ablation on the behavioral channel. Cite as the nearest philosophical neighbor within comparative cognition; distinguish clearly from opp.md's formal identifiability framing.*

- **SEP "Methods in Comparative Cognition," §3.3 "Underdetermination"** (first published 2023-09-06). https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/comparative-cognition/ — States the problem in almost exactly opp.md's vocabulary but does not formalize it: *"A major challenge in comparative cognition is that claims about cognition are often underdetermined by behavioral evidence"* ... *"if it is true that 'associative hypotheses can be constructed post-hoc for every experimental outcome' (Starzak & Gray 2021), then simply finding that one's causal-reasoning hypothesis fits a particular experimental outcome will not be sufficient to accept it over the available associative hypotheses. Instead, one must appeal to other factors, like epistemic values."* The field's own resolution is **not intervention** but appeal to *simplicity/parsimony (Morgan's Canon), unification, and predictive power* — epistemic tie-breakers layered on top of the same underdetermined behavioral data, explicitly *not* a claim that the data becomes sufficient via any observational refinement. §3.4 "Signatures and Dimensions" (Taylor et al. 2022) proposes a different partial fix: broaden from success/failure on one task to a full "signature" of errors, limits, and biases, to narrow the hypothesis space — again an observational (rung-one) strategy, not interventional. *Relevance: this is the field's live, self-aware acknowledgment that its central inferential problem is underdetermination from observational behavioral data — genuinely adjacent to opp.md's claim — but it (a) treats underdetermination as reducible by more/better observational evidence and epistemic values, not as formally non-identifiable requiring a rung-jump to intervention, and (b) is about cognition-in-general (theory of mind, causal reasoning), not specifically about dyadic communicative "understanding" of a partner's signal — the paper's exact target.*

- **SEP "Animal Communication," §2.2** (Moore & Palazzolo, 2024) — as noted in §1 above, Terrace's "manipulating keys on a keyboard" worry is conceptually the sharpest expression found of the exact apparent/functional gap, applied to communication specifically rather than cognition generally. But it is presented as one contested position in an unsettled debate, not formalized, and the SEP authors explicitly decline to resolve it ("we don't propose to settle the question... here").

**Conclusion on novelty (point 4):** nothing found threatens the paper's core identifiability claim or its formal framing. The comparative cognition and philosophy-of-animal-communication literatures have independently converged on recognizing the *same underlying problem* (behavioral/observational data underdetermines the cognitive-vs-associative or comprehension-vs-manipulation distinction) but have (i) not formalized it as a general non-identifiability result, (ii) not generalized it beyond animal cognition to a claim spanning AI/neuroscience/philosophy, and (iii) resolved it in their own domain via epistemic-value tie-breaking and mechanistic (neural) decomposition rather than via the intervention/ablation route opp.md prescribes — even though, per §3 above, that same field also happens to have the most mature ablation/playback culture of any of the paper's four target fields. That combination (recognizes the underdetermination, doesn't formalize it, doesn't unify it across fields, yet already practices something close to the remedy in its playback methodology) is worth stating explicitly in the paper as the single most informative data point from this scan: comparative cognition is simultaneously the field that most clearly *anticipates* the diagnosis and most clearly *already applies* something like the prescribed cure, without ever stating the general principle connecting the two.
