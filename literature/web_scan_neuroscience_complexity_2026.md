Web research scan conducted 2026-08-13. Scope: single-neuron/dendritic computation, connectomics + theories of brain complexity, information-theoretic accounts of "encoded but unused" neural information, and the state of hyperscanning/inter-brain synchrony research (2023-2026), with a specific eye toward whether anyone has already argued that synchrony needs an interventional/ablation control to count as evidence of functional coupling. Compiled via web search + fetch; not a systematic review — treat as a scan for leads, not exhaustive coverage.

## 1. Single-neuron and dendritic computation

**Baseline result (2021, for context).** Beniaguev, D., Segev, I., & London, M. (2021). "Single cortical neurons as deep artificial neural networks." *Neuron*, 109(17), 2727-2739. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0896627321005018 (preprint: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/613141v2, code: https://github.com/SelfishGene/neuron_as_deep_net). A temporally-convolutional deep network with 5-8 layers was needed to replicate the millisecond-resolution I/O function of a biophysically detailed layer-5 cortical pyramidal cell; removing NMDA-receptor nonlinearities collapsed the required network to a single hidden layer. Weight-matrix analysis suggested dendritic synaptic integration works like pattern-matching against spatiotemporal templates. *Relevance:* this is the anchor result the task asked to follow up on — everything below is downstream of it.

- Beniaguev, D., et al. "Single Biological Neurons as Temporally Precise Spatio-Temporal Pattern Recognizers." arXiv:2309.15090 (2023). https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.15090. A review/extension from the same group reframing the single-neuron-as-DNN result as "neurons as pattern recognizers" — positions the finding as a general framework rather than a one-off result. *Relevance:* shows the 2021 claim is being consolidated into a research program, not walked back.
- Chavlis, S., & Poirazi, P. (2025). "Dendrites endow artificial neural networks with accurate, robust and parameter-efficient learning." *Nature Communications*. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-56297-9 (preprint arXiv:2404.03708). Dendritic-inspired ANNs (structured, restricted sampling per unit rather than all-to-all) match or beat standard ANNs on image classification with far fewer trainable parameters, and are more overfitting-resistant; most dendritic units end up responding to multiple classes rather than specializing. *Relevance:* independent (non-Segev-lab) confirmation that dendritic nonlinearity/structure is a real computational resource, not an artifact of one model.
- Chavlis, S., & Poirazi, P. (2026). "A Dendro-Centric View of Cognition in the Behaving Brain." *Annual Review of Neuroscience*, 49, 79-103. https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-neuro-090325-115846. Review synthesizing in vivo evidence (behaving animals, not just slice/model work) that active dendritic events — local nonlinear spikes, branch-specific plasticity — are functionally necessary for perception, action, and memory, not epiphenomenal. *Relevance:* best current single survey of "how much does a neuron actually compute" from data, not just modeling.
- Agrawal, A., & Buice, M. A. (2025). "Bounds on the computational complexity of neurons due to dendritic morphology." NeurIPS 2025 (poster). https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/d9e56606b1bd3095fbb4241c4e15e420-Paper-Conference.pdf (preprint: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.11.664448v1). Formal/empirical result: using random Boolean-function learnability as a probe, there's a sharp phase transition — below a critical input dimension, a dendritic unit can learn almost any random function; above it, learnability collapses. The critical dimension is well-predicted by the size of the dendritic arbor. *Relevance:* this is the closest thing found to a quantitative "capacity" number for a single neuron — it's a computational-complexity-theory bound tied to morphology, not just an empirical fit-quality claim like the 2021 paper.
- "Dendritic morphology and synaptic nonlinearities enhance functional complexity in human cortical neurons," *PNAS* (2025/2026), https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2533168123 — found via search but WebFetch timed out twice (network error, not a paywall signal) and I could not confirm authors/numbers directly; title and venue suggest it's a close cousin of the Beniaguev/Chavlis line, specifically on human (vs. rodent) cortical neurons. **Flagging as unverified** — worth a direct follow-up read rather than citing on the strength of search snippets alone.
- NeurIPS 2025's "Scaling Laws and Tradeoffs in Recurrent Networks of Expressive Neurons," arXiv:2605.12049, turned up in searches but was not fetched — flagged as a lead, not reviewed.

**Gap:** I did not find a single agreed-upon "bits of capacity per neuron" number analogous to a channel-capacity estimate — the field's answer to "how much does one neuron compute" is currently "equivalent to a several-layer DNN for its specific I/O task" (Beniaguev) plus "bounded by dendritic-arbor size in a Boolean-learnability sense" (Agrawal & Buice), not a unified scalar. Report this as the honest state of the estimate rather than picking one number.

## 2. Complexity of the brain / cortical circuits: connectomics and theories

### Connectomics progress

- Dorkenwald, S., et al. (FlyWire Consortium). "Neuronal wiring diagram of an adult brain." *Nature* (2024). https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07686-5. First complete adult-brain connectome of any complex animal: 139,255 proofread neurons, >50 million synaptic connections, from a female *Drosophila melanogaster*, assembled from ~7,000 EM sections over a ~10-year international effort (Princeton, MRC-LMB Cambridge, Univ. of Vermont, Univ. of Cambridge). 8,453 annotated cell types (4,581 newly described beyond the earlier partial hemibrain connectome). Companion coverage: https://news.berkeley.edu/2024/10/02/researchers-simulate-an-entire-fly-brain-on-a-laptop-is-a-human-brain-next/, https://mrclmb.ac.uk/news-events/articles/whole-brain-connectome-of-fruit-fly-is-most-complex-brain-ever-mapped/. *Relevance:* the current ceiling on "we have the full wiring diagram" — still 3-4 orders of magnitude below mammalian cortex.
- MICrONS Consortium. "Functional connectomics spanning multiple areas of mouse visual cortex." *Nature* (2025). https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08790-w (PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11981939/). Dense calcium imaging of ~75,000 neurons across primary + higher visual areas in an awake mouse, co-registered with an EM reconstruction of >200,000 cells and ~500 million synapses (>1 petabyte of data) — the first dataset combining both function (activity) and structure (wiring) at this scale for the same neurons.
- Ding, Z., et al. / MICrONS. "Functional connectomics reveals general wiring rule in mouse visual cortex." *Nature*, 640, 459-469 (2025). https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08840-3 (PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11981947/). Neurons with similar response tuning are preferentially wired together ("like-to-like" connectivity), consistently across layers, areas, and feedback connections — and this rule independently emerges in ANNs trained on the same tasks, and matters for their performance. *Relevance:* structural evidence that functional similarity and physical connectivity are tightly coupled in cortex — a possible bridge concept for arguing what "genuine functional coupling" should look like structurally, as opposed to superficial correlation.
- Shapson-Coe, A., et al. "A connectomic study of a petascale fragment of human cerebral cortex" ("H01"), published in *Science* (2024) after the original 2021 bioRxiv preprint/Google blog release. https://vcg.seas.harvard.edu/publications/a-connectomic-study-of-a-petascale-fragment-of-human-cerebral-cortex, https://h01-release.storage.googleapis.com/landing.html, https://cbs.fas.harvard.edu/2024/05/12/human-cortex-connectome/. 1.4 petabytes of 4nm-resolution EM imaging of ~1 mm³ of human temporal cortex; tens of thousands of reconstructed neurons, ~183 million annotated synapses, ~100 fully proofread cells. This remains the largest and most detailed human brain tissue sample reconstructed to date, but is still a small fragment (not whole-brain, not even whole-column) — human whole-brain connectomics remains far off.
- "State of Brain Emulation Report 2025," arXiv:2510.15745, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.15745 — a broader survey of where connectome-to-emulation efforts stand across species; flagged as a lead for anyone wanting the full landscape, not deeply reviewed here.

**Gap:** human whole-brain (or even whole-cortex) connectomics is not remotely close; current human data is a ~1mm³ fragment. Scaling arguments from fly (139k neurons) to mouse cortex (~10⁷-10⁸ neurons, partially mapped in a functional/structural hybrid dataset) to human cortex (~10¹⁰ neurons, only a fragment mapped) should be treated as many orders of magnitude apart, not a smooth trend.

### Theories of what makes brain activity "complex" — IIT vs. GWT/GNWT

- **The adversarial collaboration.** Cogitate Consortium (multi-site, Templeton World Charity Foundation-funded; pre-registered by both IIT and GNWT theory proponents jointly with the consortium). "Adversarial testing of global neuronal workspace and integrated information theories of consciousness." *Nature*, 642(8066), 133-142 (2025). https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08888-1 (PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12137136/; original pre-registration: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.06.23.546249.full.pdf; PLOS protocol paper: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0268577). n=256 participants, tested with fMRI, MEG, and intracranial EEG on three pre-registered, theory-derived predictions about (a) where conscious content is represented, (b) whether representations are sustained over time, and (c) how regions communicate. Result: **neither theory's predictions survived intact.** IIT's prediction of sustained posterior synchronization during conscious perception was not supported; GNWT's prediction of content decodable in prefrontal cortex was only partly supported, but GNWT-predicted high-frequency posterior→frontal (not posterior→posterior, as IIT predicted) connectivity was found. Good synthesis pieces: Quanta (Aug 2023, on the earlier preregistration/early data): https://www.quantamagazine.org/what-a-contest-of-consciousness-theories-really-proved-20230824/; GeekWire (2025): https://www.geekwire.com/2025/new-clues-roots-consciousness/; independent write-up: https://theconsciousness.ai/posts/cogitate-consortium-adversarial-iit-gnw-consciousness-nature-2025/. *Relevance:* this is the highest-profile real-world instance of exactly the epistemic move Ashok's paper is built on — trying to adjudicate a theoretical construct (here, "consciousness correlates," there, "coupling/understanding") using rigorous but purely observational (non-interventional) neuroimaging, and getting an ambiguous result. Worth citing as a cautionary parallel: even a maximally rigorous, pre-registered, multi-theory adversarial observational test failed to cleanly discriminate between structurally different theories — arguably strengthening the paper's argument that observation alone under-identifies these constructs.
- **The "pseudoscience" open letter.** Fleming, J., et al. (~124 signatories incl. Bernard Baars, Daniel Dennett, Joseph LeDoux). Open letter (PsyArXiv, published/publicized ~Sept 15-16, 2023) labeling IIT "pseudoscience," on grounds it commits to panpsychism and (per critics) is untestable as a whole. Nature News coverage: "Consciousness theory slammed as 'pseudoscience'" (2023), https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-023-02971-1; rebuttals from Christoph Koch and Anil Seth, e.g. https://nautil.us/the-worth-of-wild-ideas-399097; formal underpinning argument: Doerig et al.'s "Unfolding Argument" (causal-structure theories like IIT are, per this argument, either untestable or false). *Relevance:* background on why IIT is contested independent of the 2025 adversarial-collaboration result — useful if the paper wants a sentence noting IIT specifically (not just "theories of consciousness" generically) faces a testability critique structurally similar to the paper's own non-identifiability argument, though aimed at a different target (falsifiability of the construct itself, not observation vs. intervention).

**Gap:** I did not find any theory of brain "complexity" explicitly built around an intervention/observation distinction analogous to the paper's non-identifiability theorem — IIT and GNWT are both fundamentally computed from observational neural data (with IIT's Φ formally requiring full causal/interventional knowledge of the system's transition probabilities in principle, but in practice always estimated from correlational proxies in empirical work). This gap itself may be worth a sentence in the paper: even the most causally-flavored theory of consciousness (IIT, whose Φ is defined over causal structure) is in practice tested only observationally.

## 3. Information-theoretic accounts of neural coding: "information present but not used"

This is the section most directly relevant to the paper's core empirical result (near-perfect decodable information in a receiver's hidden layer with ~0% of it used behaviorally). I found a real, established neuroscience literature on exactly this encoding/readout gap:

- Stringer, C., Pachitariu, M., Steinmetz, N., Carandini, M., & Harris, K. D. "High-dimensional geometry of population responses in visual cortex." *Nature*, 571, 361-365 (2019). https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1346-5 (preprint: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/374090v2.full; data/code: https://github.com/MouseLand/stringer-pachitariu-et-al-2018b). ~10,000 mouse V1 neurons recorded responding to 2,800 natural images; population responses are high-dimensional with principal-component variance following an unexpected 1/n power law (a specific quantitative signature of "just barely" staying differentiable/robust to noise while maximizing information). *Relevance:* establishes the "encoded" side of the ledger with hard numbers.
- Related/downstream claim, surfaced repeatedly in search but not independently pinned to one exact paper/DOI in this pass: information encoded across populations as large as ~20,000 mouse V1 neurons does not saturate, and the visual acuity that could in principle be *decoded* from that population activity **exceeds the mouse's actual behavioral visual acuity by roughly 10-fold.** This is very likely from Stringer/Pachitariu/Carandini-lab follow-up work (possibly "High-precision coding in visual cortex," Stringer et al., *Cell* 2021, https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(21)00373-1, which is in the same lab's line and about precision/acuity limits) but **I was not able to fully verify the exact 10x claim against a specific paper/figure in this pass — flag as needing a direct-read confirmation before citing the "10x" number in the paper.** The qualitative claim (decodable info >> behaviorally used info in mouse visual cortex) is well corroborated across multiple search snippets even if the precise source needs pinning down.
- Grootswagers, T., Cichy, R. M., & Carlson, T. A. "Finding decodable information that can be read out in behaviour." *NeuroImage* (2018/2019). https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1053811918305299 (preprint: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/248583v3.full). Using MEG + a "distance-to-bound" approach, the authors show decodable category information is present broadly across visual cortex, but only a *subset* of that decodable information — concentrated in anterior ventral temporal cortex — actually predicts trial-by-trial categorization behavior. Explicit partial dissociation between "decodable" and "behaviorally used." *Relevance:* closest direct human-neuroscience analog to the paper's empirical claim — a rigorous, named method for asking exactly "is this information used, or just present," with a clean positive result (some regions carry decodable-but-behaviorally-inert information).
- Kaufman, M. T., Churchland, M. M., Ryu, S. I., & Shenoy, K. V. "Cortical activity in the null space: permitting preparation without movement." *Nature Neuroscience* (2014). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24487233/. Establishes the "output-null subspace" concept: motor cortex activity during movement preparation lives largely in a subspace that mathematically cancels out at the level of the (linear) behavioral readout, so the monkey can "think about" the movement without producing it. Follow-on framework: "The population doctrine in cognitive neuroscience" (Neuron, 2021), https://www.cell.com/neuron/fulltext/S0896-6273(21)00521-3, which generalizes null-space thinking as a reason "a large fraction of neural activity is not easily accounted for by external variables." *Relevance:* this is the most conceptually mature "information present, not used" framework in mainstream systems neuroscience — decades of motor-cortex work built entirely around the idea that population activity contains a behaviorally-inert (null) subspace and a behaviorally-potent (output-potent) subspace. Strongly worth citing as the closest existing theoretical vocabulary neuroscience has for the paper's finding — though note it's about *within-one-brain* readout gating (motor output), not about a second agent's receiver hidden layer, so the analogy is structural, not identical.

**Assessment:** the "information present but behaviorally near-unused" phenomenon is well-established and named in neuroscience (null space / output-potent vs. output-null; decodable-but-not-read-out information) — this is good news for the paper's plausibility (it's not a weird artifact of the toy RL setup) but also means the paper should probably cite this literature explicitly as precedent, both to strengthen credibility and to preempt a reviewer citing it as "this is already known."

## 4. Hyperscanning / inter-brain synchrony (2023-2026) — and the novelty question

### State of the field

- Schilbach, L., & Redcay, E. "Synchrony Across Brains." *Annual Review of Psychology*, 76, 883-911 (2025; first published online Oct 2024). https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-psych-080123-101149. Current authoritative field review. *Relevance:* best single starting point for "what do neuroscientists currently claim IBS measures" — treats synchrony as an index of shared attention/engagement/coordination, generally still framed as correlational.
- Systematic reviews confirm IBS is now applied broadly: autism/social-impairment (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12586996/, https://academic.oup.com/psyrad/article/doi/10.1093/psyrad/kkaf025/8257567), stress/anxiety/depression (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12564765/), naturalistic seminar/learning contexts predicting relational satisfaction and task performance (https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2025.1705767/full). All are correlational: synchrony level is measured and correlated with an outcome measure, not manipulated.

### Methodological critiques (2023-2025)

- Zimmermann, M., Schultz-Nielsen, K., Dumas, G., & Konvalinka, I. "Arbitrary methodological decisions skew inter-brain synchronization estimates in hyperscanning-EEG studies." *Imaging Neuroscience* (2024/2025). https://direct.mit.edu/imag/article/doi/10.1162/imag_a_00350/124910 (PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12290587/). Shows common circular-correlation implementations produce large spurious fluctuations in IBS estimates, and short (<1s) epoch windows inflate apparent synchrony even with no real interaction — i.e., a chunk of the literature's "synchrony" may be a preprocessing artifact. *Relevance:* independent methodological grounds (separate from the paper's identifiability argument) for distrusting raw synchrony numbers.
- **Confirmed (title, authors, venue now verified via a second search pass after repeated direct-fetch timeouts on ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/nature.com — full text still not directly read):** Varlet, M., & Grootswagers, T. (2024). "Measuring information alignment in hyperscanning research with representational analyses: moving beyond interbrain synchrony." *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience*, 18, 1385624. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11306121/ ; https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2024.1385624/epub ; PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39118818/. Using EEG hyperscanning + simulations, shows interbrain synchrony has **low sensitivity to information alignment** across people — synchrony stays largely unchanged even when the experimenters manipulate whether two people are seeing the *same* or *different* things at the same time. Proposes representational similarity analysis across brains as a fix, measuring whether the *content/structure* of information is shared, not just whether oscillatory phase/amplitude tracks together. Note: Grootswagers is also a co-author of the "Finding decodable information that can be read out in behaviour" paper in Section 3 — the same "decodable-but-not-behaviorally/representationally-aligned" logic applied first within one brain, then across two. **This is the strongest, most directly on-point lead found in this scan** — it is explicitly an argument that synchrony is a poor proxy for actual shared representational content, very close to (though not identical to) the paper's "synchrony ≠ functional coupling" point. It still stops short of an interventional/ablation design (it manipulates stimulus content, not the coupling channel itself) — see novelty assessment below.
- Markus, A., & Shamay-Tsoory, S. G. "Hyperscanning: from inter-brain coupling to causality." *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience*, 18, 1497034 (2024). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11599244/. **Confirmed by direct fetch.** Explicitly names the problem: "it is not yet clear whether inter-brain coupling triggers behavioral changes or if dyadic behavior creates inter-brain coupling." Proposes three fixes: (1) dyadic neurofeedback — real-time feedback lets participants deliberately try to modulate their own coupling, an experimental-intervention design; (2) Granger causality; (3) structural equation modeling (both statistical, not interventional). **The paper does NOT explicitly propose ablation studies**, and does not frame the problem in terms of "synchrony is a poor identifier of functional coupling" the way the paper's non-identifiability framing does — it frames it as a directionality/causality problem (which variable drives which), closer to "does A cause B or B cause A" than to "does observing A,B at all let you infer whether coupling exists."
- Related "epiphenomenon" concern (surfaced via search, not directly fetched): field has long worried IBS might be an artifact of two brains independently processing the same shared stimulus/task rather than true interpersonal coupling. Response method: "pseudopair" analysis — comparing real interacting dyads against pseudo-dyads (recordings from non-interacting individuals who did the same task independently), showing 2023 work finds stronger coupling in real pairs. This is the field's closest existing move toward the paper's "ablation control" logic — pseudopairs are a kind of control condition, but they control for the *stimulus*, not for the *coupling channel itself* (i.e., they don't ablate/perturb the interaction to test whether the synchrony is functionally load-bearing).

### Novelty assessment (direct answer to the task's key question)

I did **not** find a hyperscanning paper that makes the paper's specific move: designing an *interventional or ablation* test of whether inter-brain synchrony is functionally load-bearing for the interaction it's claimed to support (e.g., artificially disrupting/injecting synchrony independent of task content, then checking whether the behavioral/task outcome tracks the manipulation vs. the underlying task variables). What exists instead, ranked by closeness:

1. **Closest in spirit:** Varlet & Grootswagers (2024, Frontiers in Human Neuroscience, confirmed above) — argues synchrony is insensitive to information content, using a same/different-stimulus manipulation. This is a manipulation of *stimulus content*, not of the *coupling channel*, but it is the most similar existing critique.
2. **Closest in method:** dyadic neurofeedback (Markus & Shamay-Tsoory 2024) — an actual intervention on coupling itself, but aimed at establishing directionality/causality between coupling and behavior, not at testing whether decodable synchrony is functionally used at all (i.e., it doesn't test the "synchrony present but unused" possibility).
3. **Closest in control logic:** pseudopair designs — a control condition, but controls for shared-stimulus confound, not for whether the coupling itself does any work.

None of these frame the problem the way the paper does (non-identifiability of functional coupling from purely observational synchrony measures, requiring intervention to identify). This looks like a genuine gap the paper's framing could fill in the hyperscanning literature specifically. Varlet & Grootswagers (2024) is the closest existing precedent and should be read in full and likely cited — it establishes that synchrony ≠ information alignment using an observational/stimulus-manipulation design, but does not go the extra step to an interventional/ablation test of the coupling channel itself, which is where the paper's contribution would still be distinct. Full text of that paper was not directly read in this session (repeated network timeouts on ncbi.nlm.nih.gov); citation and claims above are corroborated by two independent search passes but should be confirmed against the full text before being relied on for a precise novelty claim in the paper.
