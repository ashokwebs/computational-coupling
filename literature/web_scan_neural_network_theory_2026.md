## 1. Probing classifiers: "decodability ≠ use" in single-model interpretability

This is thoroughly established prior art — the paper's Proposition 1 is a *specific instance*, in a two-agent communication setting with a named confounder, of a general point this literature has been making since 2019 about single models. That should be framed explicitly as a strength (independent convergent evidence from a different research tradition), not hidden.

1. **Hewitt, J. & Liang, P. (2019). "Designing and Interpreting Probes with Control Tasks." EMNLP-IJCNLP 2019 (Best Paper Runner-Up).** https://aclanthology.org/D19-1275/ · https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03368
   Introduces "control tasks" — word-to-random-label mappings solvable only by probe memorization, not by the representation — to test probe *selectivity*. Shows popular probes on ELMo are not selective: high probe accuracy on a linguistic task doesn't distinguish "representation encodes this" from "probe is powerful enough to learn it from nothing." Finds dropout regularization fails to fix this; other regularizers help.
   *Relevance: foundational — establishes that probe accuracy alone is not evidence of encoding, let alone use. Adjacent, not competing: it's about whether a probe's success reflects the representation vs. the probe's own capacity, a different (earlier) link in the same causal chain that ends at this paper's use/decodability split.*

2. **Belinkov, Y. (2022). "Probing Classifiers: Promises, Shortcomings, and Advances." Computational Linguistics 48(1), 207–219.** https://aclanthology.org/2022.cl-1.7/ (DOI 10.1162/coli_a_00422)
   Survey/critical review of the probing-classifier methodology across NLP interpretability. Catalogs known shortcomings (probe capacity confounds, lack of causal guarantees, correlational-not-causal findings) and surveys "advances" that had emerged by 2022 to address them — including counterfactual/interventional variants of probing.
   *Relevance: the most direct pre-2023 articulation of "probes tell you what's decodable, not what's used" as a named, general methodological critique — squarely the single-model analogue of this paper's core distinction. Strongly supportive prior art; explicitly cite as the established version of the problem this paper demonstrates empirically in a two-agent system.*

3. **Arora, A., Jurafsky, D. & Potts, C. (2024). "CausalGym: Benchmarking Causal Interpretability Methods on Linguistic Tasks."** https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12560 (Stanford)
   Benchmark adapting SyntaxGym to test whether interpretability methods can *causally* affect model behavior, not just correlate with it. Compares linear probing against Distributed Alignment Search (DAS) on Pythia models (14M–6.9B); DAS outperforms correlational probing at causal efficacy. Used DAS to show two linguistic phenomena (NPI licensing, filler-gap dependencies) are learned in discrete stages in pythia-1b.
   *Relevance: concrete post-2022 example of the field's "fix" — building benchmarks specifically to grade methods on causal-efficacy, not decoding accuracy. Supportive, adjacent.*

4. **Jin, C. & Rinard, M. (2024). "Latent Causal Probing: A Formal Perspective on Probing with Causal Models of Data." COLM 2024.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13765
   Formalizes probing using structural causal models: given an SCM explaining how training text was generated, asks whether an LM represents the SCM's *latent* (never-observed) causal variables. Applies this to a synthetic grid-world navigation task where the true causal structure is known, giving unusually strong ground truth for probing claims.
   *Relevance: a formal (not just empirical) treatment of what a probe result can and cannot license about representation content. Adjacent — theory-side complement to this paper's empirical demonstration.*

5. **Joshi, S., Mueller, A., Klindt, D., Brendel, W., Reizinger, P. & Sridhar, D. (2026). "Position: Causality Is Key for Interpretability Claims to Generalise." ICML 2026 poster.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16698
   Position paper arguing interpretability findings routinely fail to generalize because they conflate rungs of Pearl's causal hierarchy: "observations of model behaviour support only associational claims; interventions enable cause-effect claims, but not necessarily predictions of model behaviour; counterfactuals... are often unverifiable." Proposes causal representation learning + the hierarchy as a diagnostic framework for matching interpretability claims to the evidence that actually supports them.
   *Relevance: HIGH — this is a 2026 position paper making almost exactly this paper's meta-point ("you are measuring the wrong rung") but applied to interpretability methodology broadly, not to communication/understanding specifically. Worth reading in full; it's a natural companion citation and gives the paper's rung-language extra field-wide legitimacy. Not a scoop — no communication-channel case study, no confounder-in-communication argument — but close enough on the abstract "which rung does your evidence license" point that Ashok should check it doesn't already state Proposition-1-style conclusions in passing.*

6. **Lin, Z. & Liu, F. (2026, May 8). "Position: Mechanistic Interpretability Must Disclose Identification Assumptions for Causal Claims."** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08012
   Audits 10 mechanistic-interpretability papers across four methodological strands; finds *none* has a dedicated identification-assumptions section, and that validation metrics (faithfulness, completeness, ablation effects) are routinely reported as if they were causal support without stating what would make them identifying. Proposes a disclosure norm: state whether a claim is causal, name the identification strategy, enumerate assumptions, stress-test one, state what changes if an assumption fails.
   *Relevance: directly reinforces this paper's central methodological argument (identification requires explicit assumptions; validation ≠ identification) but pointed at mech-interp practice rather than communication/understanding. Strongly supportive, not competing — could be cited as evidence the identification-disclosure problem this paper's Theorem 2 addresses is a live, named concern elsewhere in ML right now.*

7. **Sharkey, L. et al. (2025). "Open Problems in Mechanistic Interpretability."** https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16496 (Apollo Research)
   Field-survey of open problems; explicitly names "probes detect correlations instead of causal variables" as a core limitation, alongside the overfitting/spurious-correlation risk from high-dimensional activations, and lists causal methods (DAS, causal probing, attribution, concept erasure) as the fixes, noting these require more specialized data/compute than probing.
   *Relevance: another 2025 field-level restatement of "probes reveal presence, not use," confirming this is now consensus framing within mech-interp, not a fringe complaint. Supportive.*

8. A 2025 arXiv paper on **causal probing reliability** (submitted to ICLR 2025, exact title not confirmed in snippets — likely "Measuring the Reliability of Causal Probing Methods: Tradeoffs, Limitations, and the Plight of Nullifying Interventions," OpenReview id Ku1tUKnAnC) and its ~2025 IJCNLP companion "How Reliable are Causal Probing Interventions?" (https://aclanthology.org/2025.ijcnlp-long.47.pdf) build a framework to compare completeness/selectivity across causal-probing method families (linear vs. nonlinear, counterfactual vs. nullifying interventions).
   *Relevance: shows the field has moved to interrogating the causal-probing *fixes* themselves, one level deeper than the original decodability critique. Adjacent, minor.*

9. **"Causality ≠ Decodability, and Vice Versa: Lessons from Interpreting Counting ViTs."** https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09794 (title/topic confirmed via search snippet; full text not retrievable this session, WebFetch to arxiv.org failed with DNS timeout on every attempt — **flagged as unread, needs a follow-up fetch**).
   From search snippets: studies counting behavior in Vision Transformers and finds *both* directions of the dissociation — final-layer object tokens are highly decodable via probes but activation patching shows they don't causally affect predictions; conversely, middle-layer tokens flip predictions under patching despite probes recovering little information from them.
   *Relevance: HIGH and needs direct verification — the title alone states almost exactly this paper's thesis ("causality ≠ decodability") as a general principle, demonstrated in a single-model vision task. This is single-model/ViT/counting, not two-agent/communication/RL, so it doesn't preempt Proposition 1's specific claim (which is about a confound-driven dissociation *between two agents mediated by a convention*), but the title's generality is close enough that Ashok should read the full paper before finalizing framing — at minimum it should be cited as the closest single-model analogue, and the Related Work section should state explicitly why the two-agent/confounder setting is a different (and, the paper argues, more fundamental) case than a single model's internal information routing.*

**Gap:** none of the probing literature above frames its fix as anything other than "use causal/interventional probing methods instead of/alongside correlational probes" (DAS, activation patching, causal mediation, concept erasure). None of it reaches for Pearl's causal-hierarchy vocabulary of *non-identifiability from observational data* the way this paper's Theorem 1/2 does, except the two 2026 position papers (#5, #6) — and even those don't derive an impossibility result or a confounder-based non-identifiability proof; they argue for better disclosure/methodology, not for a structural, provable limit. That gap (rigorous non-identifiability proof, not just a call for better methods) is where this paper's Theorem 1/2 sit that the probing literature doesn't occupy.

## 2. Emergent multi-agent communication, 2023–2026

1. **Lowe, R., Foerster, J., Boureau, Y-L., Pineau, J. & Dauphin, Y. (2019). "On the Pitfalls of Measuring Emergent Communication." AAMAS 2019.** https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.05168 (already cited in `paper2/`)
   Already read in full per `handoff.md` §10. Introduces positive signalling (speaker message depends on observation) vs. positive listening (receiver behavior depends on message), and the Speaker-Consistency (SC) vs. Causal Influence of Communication (CIC) metrics, with SC 0.19–0.54 vs. CIC at floor — the closest prior empirical demonstration of the same decodable-but-unused dissociation, but traced to an architectural confound (shared hidden layers) rather than a convention confound, and with no oracle/unbottlenecked control. Confirmed by `novelty_assessment.md` §6 as read and distinguished.

2. **"Do Latent Channels Actually Communicate? A Causal Audit of Latent Multi-Agent LLM Communication." arXiv:2607.26773 (~2026-07). Authors: Huixiang Zhang and one co-author (full author list not resolved this session — WebFetch to arxiv.org failed).** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26773
   From abstract snippet: studies "latent relay" between Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B on GSM8K, ARC-C, MATH-500 — LLM agents passing continuous internal representations instead of text. Explicitly argues "greater representational capacity does not establish that the receiver uses task-relevant information" and that "end-task performance alone... cannot reveal whether an observed effect depends on message presence, content... or information supplied by a separate agent." Introduces a causal audit via controlled message replacement at the point the message enters the receiver, yielding five measurements: encoded sender information, receiver sensitivity to message presence/identity, task value of example-specific content, and value added by a separate agent. Explicitly cites Lowe et al.'s positive-signalling/positive-listening framing and extends it to a counterfactual, patching-based measure ("listening can be made causal by scoring a message by the counterfactual shift it induces in the receiver's policy").
   *Relevance: HIGHEST novelty risk found in this scan. This is the same question (decodable capacity ≠ causal receiver-use) applied to a live multi-agent communication channel in 2026, using causal-audit language extremely close to this paper's framing. Distinguishing factors, to verify by reading the full text: (a) LLM latent-relay setting, not learned discrete RL communication with a trained sender policy; (b) no confounder-based non-identifiability argument — it's a measurement-methodology paper, not a proof that the quantity is unidentifiable from observational data; (c) no oracle/infinite-bandwidth/zero-channel control (i.e., it doesn't test "if you just hand over the true state directly, does behavior change" — the paper's decisive move). If those three distinctions hold up under a full read, the paper's claim survives, but this should be added to `novelty_assessment.md` and read in full before the next revision — it is close enough on the framing/motivation that a referee is likely to ask about it.*

3. **Emergent Language: A Survey and Taxonomy (Peters, J. et al., 2024/2025). Autonomous Agents and Multi-Agent Systems (Springer).** https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02645 · https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10458-025-09691-y
   Systematic review of 181 publications (search conducted June 2024) building a metrics-driven taxonomy of emergent-language evaluation, explicitly organized around Lowe et al.'s positive signalling / positive listening distinction as a foundational split. Confirms these remain the field's standard vocabulary through 2024–2025.
   *Relevance: useful as the up-to-date field map — confirms no competing framework has displaced Lowe et al.'s distinction, and gives a citable source for "the field's own survey still treats this as open." Supportive, not competing.*

4. **MAGI / graph information bottleneck papers, and "Bandwidth-Efficient Multi-Agent Communication through Information Bottleneck and Vector Quantization" (Farooq, A. & Iqbal, K., 2026-02). arXiv:2602.02035.**
   These (and the AAAI 2024 "graph information bottleneck" MARL papers) treat communication design as an optimization problem: maximize mutual information between message and optimal action while minimizing message-size/leakage. Report large performance gains (e.g., 181.8% over no-communication baseline while cutting bandwidth 41.4%) — i.e., they *build* systems where MI-with-action correlates with task performance, by construction/optimization pressure, rather than testing whether MI-with-representation implies behavioral use in a system not specifically optimized for that link.
   *Relevance: this is the field's dominant IB-for-communication use pattern — see §3 gap below. Adjacent, not competing; none of these papers report or investigate a decodable-but-unused failure mode, because their training objective directly ties MI to the action, sidestepping the question this paper raises about *architectures not specifically so optimized* (ordinary policy-gradient RL with an auxiliary decoding loss, as in this paper's setup).*

5. **Reward-Independent Messaging for Decentralized MARL (2025-05).** https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21985
   Proposes MARL-CPC linking messages to state inference (collective predictive coding) rather than treating messages purely as part of the action space; targets non-cooperative/reward-independent settings.
   *Relevance: minor, tangential — different problem (decentralized/non-cooperative comm design), not a decodability/use dissociation study.*

**Gap, explicit:** No 2023–2026 paper found runs an **infinite-bandwidth / zero-noise oracle control** on a multi-agent RL communication system — i.e., removing the channel entirely and handing the receiver the sender's true state directly, to test whether *any* amount of "communication" would help. This appears to be genuinely novel to this paper within the emergent-communication literature (Lowe et al. get closest but stop at CIC/SC metrics on the trained channel; the 2026 LLM latent-audit paper stops at controlled message replacement, not full channel removal). This is the strongest specific-novelty finding of the scan and should be stated plainly in the paper's related-work / novelty framing.

## 3. Information bottleneck theory and channel capacity

1. **Tishby, N. & Zaslavsky, N. (and the broader IB literature; Tishby & Pereira & Bialek 1999 original).** Background only, well-known.
   *Relevance: background.*

2. **"A Critical Review of Information Bottleneck Theory and its Applications to Deep Learning."** https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.04405 (pre-2023, but relevant background) and follow-on critiques found via search: binning sensitivity, ReLU networks not showing the predicted compression phase, MI being infinite/trivial in deterministic networks, and — most relevant — the finding that "controlling mutual information is one way to control performance in deep learning, although it is not the necessary way," and that "mutual information... is not sufficient" as a measure of what representations a network actually uses.
   *Relevance: this is the closest thing found to an existing pushback on "MI/capacity as sufficient for X," but it's aimed at IB's original claim about generalization/compression dynamics inside a single network, not at inter-agent communication specifically. It supports this paper's general skepticism of information-theoretic sufficiency claims but doesn't make the communication-specific argument.*

3. **Graph-information-bottleneck / bandwidth-efficient MARL papers (see §2.4).** These are the field's actual 2023–2026 intersection of IB theory and inter-agent channel capacity. They uniformly use IB as a *design/regularization* tool (compress the message while keeping it maximally predictive of action), not as a *measurement* framework someone might mistake for evidence of communication having occurred. None frames channel capacity or MI-across-the-channel as *sufficient* evidence of communication in the sense this paper critiques (i.e., nobody explicitly claims "if I(M;U) is high, communication has occurred" and then gets corrected) — they simply build systems where that link is true by construction.

**Gap, explicit and important:** the scan found **no paper, in the IB tradition or elsewhere, that explicitly treats information-theoretic channel capacity or cross-agent mutual information as *sufficient* for "communication" and then gets challenged on it** — nor any paper that pushes back on that equation the way this paper's Proposition 1 does. The IB-for-MARL literature avoids the issue by optimizing MI directly against the action/task objective, which is a different situation from this paper's setup (a trained sender with an auxiliary decoding loss, RL receiver not similarly optimized to use the channel). This looks like a genuinely open framing in the literature — worth stating as a gap rather than citing something that only partially covers it. If Ashok wants a stronger citation here, Kolchinsky & Wolpert's semantic-information program (already cited; §4 below) and its 2024 "Causal Leverage Density" follow-on are the nearest match, since they explicitly distinguish syntactic (Shannon) information from information with causal/semantic power — but that's a monadic (one system vs. environment) framework, not a channel-capacity-between-two-agents critique, consistent with the paper's existing novelty assessment.

4. **Bartlett, S. (2024/2025). "Causal Leverage Density: A General/Universal Approach to Semantic Information."** https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07335 (ALIFE 2024/2025 versions)
   Follow-up to Kolchinsky & Wolpert (2018), already cited in `paper2/`. Replaces K&W's viability-of-a-subsystem criterion with a more general "changes in future trajectory through phase space caused by informational interventions (erasure/scrambling)" criterion, extending semantic-information quantification to any system (not just living/self-maintaining ones).
   *Relevance: confirms K&W's framework is still actively being extended as of 2024–2025 but remains monadic (single system vs. environment) — consistent with `novelty_assessment.md`'s existing verdict that K&W and descendants don't pre-empt this paper's dyadic/confounder claim. Worth a one-line mention in the paper's related-work if K&W is discussed, to show the lineage is still monadic in its most recent form too.*

## 4. RLHF / chat-based AI evaluation validity critiques, 2023–2026

The clearest and most encouraging finding in this section: a small but real 2026 sub-literature has independently converged on almost exactly this paper's rung-1/rung-2 argument, applied to RLHF and reward modeling, using Pearl's causal-hierarchy vocabulary explicitly (not just "reward hacking" or "sycophancy").

1. **"Right for the Wrong Reasons: Epistemic Regret Minimization for LLM Causal Reasoning" (a.k.a. "...for Causal Rung Collapse in LLMs"). arXiv:2602.11675 (2026-02).** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11675
   Formalizes **"Rung Collapse"**: systematic substitution of lower-rung (associational) reasoning for higher-rung (interventional) queries in Pearl's hierarchy, where a model gets rewarded identically whether it reasoned causally or exploited a correlational shortcut, producing "Reward Entrenchment" — the flawed shortcut is reinforced because outcome-based reward cannot see *why* the answer was right. Reports GPT-4 at only 64% on causal queries, concentrated failures at the interventional rung, and much lower collapse rates for Claude Sonnet 3.5 (~1.5%) — suggesting collapse is reduced but not eliminated by scale. Explicitly states: "A training signal that separates correlation from causation is provably absent from observational data alone, which is what RLHF preference learning relies on."
   *Relevance: HIGH — this is the paper closest in spirit to point 4 of the assignment. It makes essentially the same structural argument (RLHF trains on rung-1 evidence for a rung-2 quantity, and this is provably insufficient) but scoped to LLM causal-reasoning benchmarks and chain-of-thought faithfulness, not to human-AI functional understanding/coupling broadly. Should be read in full and cited — it is independent convergent support for this paper's §7/§8 argument from a different specific application, not a scoop, since it doesn't address communication/understanding-as-such or run anything like the oracle control.*

2. **"CausalRM: Causal-Theoretic Reward Modeling for RLHF from Observational User Feedbacks."** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18736 (2026-03)
   Proposes factoring the reward model's embedding into causal factors sufficient for reward prediction vs. non-causal (reward-irrelevant) factors, explicitly to address confounding in preference data collected observationally from users.
   *Relevance: a proposed *fix* for exactly the kind of confounding this paper's framework predicts should occur in RLHF pipelines — supportive, not competing; a good citation for "here is the field independently building patches for the identifiability problem this paper names structurally."*

3. **"Factored Causal Representation Learning for Robust Reward Modeling in RLHF."** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21350 (2026-01)
   Similar aim to CausalRM — uses causal representation learning to separate genuine task-relevant reward signal from spurious/confounded correlates in reward modeling; states plainly that identifying causal mechanisms from observational RLHF data "requires stronger assumptions or additional supervision... not provided by default in standard RLHF pipelines."
   *Relevance: same as #2 — independent, convergent, supportive; not a scoop of this paper's specific claim (functional understanding/coupling is unidentifiable from RLHF-style observational preference data), since these papers target reward-model robustness/fairness rather than the understanding-vs-appearance distinction.*

4. **Jiang, E., Gjølbye, A., Zhang, Y. J. & Koyejo, S. (2026-06). "When Behavioral Safety Evaluation Fails: A Representation-Level Perspective."** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08044
   Formalizes an "audit gap": the difference between behavioral safety (what RLHF/safety evals measure) and robustness under intervention (representation-level vulnerability). Constructs "dissociated models" that behave safely on the surface while remaining vulnerable in latent space to targeted perturbation; introduces a Latent Vulnerability Score. Finds behavioral safety metrics are insufficient measures of representation-level robustness across multiple state-of-the-art aligned models.
   *Relevance: HIGH and structurally very close — this is a decodable/latent-vulnerability-vs-surface-behavior dissociation, deliberately engineered ("dissociated models") rather than found emergent, applied to LLM safety evaluation specifically rather than to communicative understanding. It is independent confirmation that "behavioral/observational evaluation of an LLM can be dissociated from what's actually happening in its representations" is a live, actively-studied 2026 concern — directly relevant to this paper's RLHF argument (point 4 of the assignment) even though its target is safety robustness, not task understanding. Should be cited as convergent evidence; not a scoop, since it's about adversarial robustness under intervention rather than the causal-ladder non-identifiability argument this paper makes about ordinary preference training.*

5. **General "reward hacking / sycophancy / unfaithful chain-of-thought" literature** (e.g., faithfulness-bias reward-model papers, "reasoning hallucination," MONICA sycophancy monitoring, thermodynamic sycophancy analyses) — found in volume but explicitly **not** using causal-ladder vocabulary; these frame the problem as reward models rewarding "plausible-sounding" over "faithful" reasoning, which is adjacent to but distinct from this paper's identifiability claim (the paper's point is not that RLHF *sometimes* rewards the wrong thing due to a training artifact, but that genuine vs. apparent understanding is *structurally unidentifiable* from the kind of data RLHF collects, as a matter of causal-inference principle, independent of any particular training bug).
   *Relevance: LOW/adjacent per the task's own framing — flagged explicitly as the "adjacent but not the same claim" category the assignment asked to distinguish from. Good contrastive citations to sharpen the paper's own distinction ("this is not a reward-hacking or sycophancy argument, it is an identifiability argument") in the Related Work/Discussion section.*

**Gap:** the causal-ladder-vocabulary RLHF critiques that do exist (#1–#3 above) are all scoped to LLM *reasoning-chain faithfulness* or *reward-model construction*, not to the broader "genuine vs. merely apparent understanding" claim this paper makes about deployed chat-based AI evaluation as a category. None of them make the four-fields argument (RLHF, hyperscanning, BBI, mind-reading-adjacent measurement all sharing the same rung-1-for-rung-2 structural flaw) — that framing appears to remain distinctively this paper's.

## Overall assessment

The paper's specific empirical claim — two RL agents, a learned discrete channel, a named convention confounder, decodability to R²≈0.90/reconstruction error 0.0017 alongside z≈0 behavioral coupling, surviving an oracle/no-channel/zero-noise control — was not found duplicated anywhere in the 2023–2026 literature scanned. The *general principle* (decodability ≠ causal use) is well-established across single-model interpretability (§1) and is being independently rediscovered in 2026 for LLM multi-agent latent channels (§2.2) and LLM safety evaluation (§4.4), which is good news framed correctly: it means the paper's specific mechanism sits inside a pattern the field is actively noticing elsewhere, strengthening rather than undermining the four-fields claim, provided the Related Work section is updated to cite this convergence explicitly rather than risk a referee finding it first.
