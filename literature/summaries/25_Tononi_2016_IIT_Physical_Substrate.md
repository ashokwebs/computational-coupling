# Research Paper Report: Integrated Information Theory: From Consciousness to Its Physical Substrate

**Authors:** Giulio Tononi, Melanie Boly, Marcello Massimini, Christof Koch
**Publication Year:** 2016
**Venue:** *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 17:450–461
**DOI/arXiv:** `10.1038/nrn.2016.44`
**Category:** (4) Integrated Information Theory (IIT)
**Role in Our Work:** **Adjacent theory / update to canon's IIT reference, not a threat.** The mature, widely-cited IIT 3.0 review; pairs with `24_Tononi_2004`.

---

## 📌 Abstract & Architecture
Updates and consolidates IIT (version 3.0) as a review, deriving the theory from five phenomenological axioms of experience (existence, composition, information, integration, exclusion) and their corresponding physical postulates on a system's causal substrate. Argues the physical substrate of consciousness must be a maximum of intrinsic cause-effect power ("maximally irreducible conceptual structure," or MICS) and discusses empirical tools derived from the theory (e.g. the perturbational complexity index, PCI) for assessing consciousness in non-communicative patients — an experimentally validated, falsifiable spinoff of otherwise abstract $\Phi$ theory.

## 🔗 Connection to Computational Coupling Theory
This is the more citable, higher-authority IIT reference for the theory paper's discussion of open question 3 (relation between $C_{\text{couple}}$ and $\Phi$) — cite this alongside or instead of the 2004 original when writing for a neuroscience audience, since it is the version most neuroscientists actually mean by "IIT" today. Practically useful methodological pointer: the Perturbational Complexity Index (PCI) described here — a TMS-evoked-response-based complexity measure — is a candidate proxy for "effective dimensionality" of a receiving brain in Prediction 1 (capacity saturates at $\min(\text{eff-dim}(i),\text{eff-dim}(j))$), and could plausibly be reused as an empirical stand-in for effective dimension in a future BBI experiment, since PCI is already validated as non-invasive and clinically deployed.
