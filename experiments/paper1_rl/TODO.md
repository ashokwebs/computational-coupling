The supervised probe that worked used batch_size=64 and a direct loss; the RL loop uses batch size 1 and an indirect, high-variance one.

The speaker gets *a* real gradient every episode, but with only one episode's worth of noisy listener-action-sampling variance behind each update, thousands of updates may still fail to average out to the correct direction within a 2000-episode budget.

  - **Complete picture, three findings together:** (1) the architecture *can* encode a near-perfect code under direct supervision: (2) under RL, the speaker never learns to encode anything (R²~0.001–0.05 regardless of batch size or entropy_coef); (3) the listener has learned to ignore the channel almost entirely (18x weaker sensitivity than to its own state). This is the textbook emergent-communication chicken-and-egg/bootstrapping failure (see this project's own canon: Foerster DIAL, Mordatch emergent compositional language, Lazaridou referential games) — the speaker has no incentive to encode a message the listener won't use, and the listener has no incentive to attend to a message the speaker isn't encoding, so neither side moves first under plain REINFORCE, regardless of batch size or exploration tuning.

- [x] **Built the auxiliary bootstrapping loss — and found (and fixed) why the first attempt at it failed too.** `train_with_aux_loss.py`: same joint RL training loop, plus a decoder that must reconstruct the speaker's goal observation from the transmitted message (the exact loss `probe_supervised_encoding.py` proved converges in ~300-450 steps standalone), added into the total loss as `aux_coef * aux_loss`.

  - **Diagnosed why:** `policy_loss`/`value_loss` use `reduction="sum"` over a ~400-timestep batch (16 episodes × ~25 steps), while `aux_loss` uses `reduction="mean"` — a ~400-1200x scale mismatch. At `aux_coef=1.0` the RL terms' gradients on the shared speaker parameters almost certainly swamped the auxiliary signal.

But `listener_message_sensitivity_kl` = 0.0704 vs. `listener_state_sensitivity_kl` = 0.9832 — the *ratio* barely improved (≈7% vs. ≈4.5% before), and most tellingly, **the real-vs-zero-message eval-return z-stat is now exactly 0.00** — completely identical task performance whether the listener receives the genuine, information-rich message or nothing at all. Despite the channel now carrying substantial real information about the goal (R²=0.76), the listener has still not learned to exploit it, even with 3x the training and a fully-converged auxiliary signal.
  - **Conclusion: the bottleneck has cleanly moved to the receiver side.** The auxiliary loss fully solved the sender-side half of the chicken-and-egg problem (the speaker now encodes a real, stable, near-fully-informative code), which decisively rules out "there's nothing to decode" as the listener's excuse. What remains is a receiver-side credit-assignment problem: plain REINFORCE on the listener isn't discovering that conditioning its action on the now-meaningful message dimensions improves reward, even when that signal has been available, stable, and strong for thousands of episodes. This is itself a well-known hard case for policy gradient methods (learning to attend to a specific input dimension is a representation-learning problem, not just a small local weight nudge) — fixing it would need its own bootstrapping aid on the receiving end (e.g. an analogous auxiliary loss that pretrains/pressures the listener to decode the message, or a supervised pretraining phase for the listener against the now-fixed speaker, before or alongside RL), which is a large enough new direction that it's the right place to stop today's chain rather than try another isolated one-off tweak.

- [x] **Built the listener's own bootstrapping aid — and it reveals an even deeper layer to this problem.** `train_with_receiver_aux.py`: a new `ListenerPolicyWithAux` shares its trunk between the existing action head and a *new* goal-reconstruction head (both fed from the same hidden features, unlike a separate frozen probe — this is the only way an auxiliary loss can actually influence the policy's real behavior). Both speaker and listener get their own auxiliary loss now (`speaker_aux_coef=200`, `listener_aux_coef=200`), 20000 episodes, `episodes_per_update=16`, `entropy_coef=0.0`:
  - Speaker encoding R² climbed even further: **0.90** (up from 0.76 with speaker-only aux).
  - **Listener's own auxiliary reconstruction loss converges beautifully: 0.0016.** This proves the goal information is now genuinely, linearly present in the listener's shared hidden representation — the same features that feed its action head.
  - **But the action head still doesn't use it.** `listener_message_sensitivity_kl` = 0.0571 vs. `listener_state_sensitivity_kl` = 1.0189 (still a large gap), and real-vs-zero eval return z-stat = **-0.04** — essentially zero, again.
  - **This is a sharper, stranger finding than before:** it's not that the information is unavailable to the listener (it demonstrably is — a separate linear head reads it out almost perfectly) — it's that the *action-selection* readout from that same hidden vector never learns to exploit it. Two projections off the identical hidden features, one succeeds completely, one doesn't move at all.
  - **Tested the entropy hypothesis: partial nudge, not a resolution.** Same dual-aux config with `entropy_coef=0.02` restored (20000 episodes): real-vs-zero z-stat moved from **-0.04 to +0.50** — a small shift in the right direction, but nowhere near significant (would want |z| ≳ 2).   - **Honest bottom line for this thread:** giving the listener's hidden features demonstrable, near-perfectly-decodable goal information (via its own auxiliary head, sharing the trunk with the action head) is *not* sufficient to make the action head itself condition its behavior on that information, even with 20000 episodes and reasonable exploration. The information is present; the policy doesn't read it out for decision-making. This is a materially different and deeper question than anything else diagnosed today — it looks less like a hyperparameter problem and more like a genuine open question about why policy-gradient action readout doesn't exploit already-available, already-disentangled features in this setup. That's a good place for a fresh session with new ideas (e.g. imitation-learning the action head directly against an oracle that has the goal; checking whether the *value* baseline is what's failing to differentiate on message, indirectly suppressing the advantage signal that would reward message-conditioned actions; or simply accepting this as a real, citable negative result about REINFORCE's limits here) rather than more same-day one-off tweaks.
