### The thing I kept explaining to people

Review 1 was twelve days ago and I have had the same conversation maybe six times since. Someone
asks what I'm working on, I say there's a paper about coupling capacity, and then two sentences
later I'm saying "well actually there's a second paper that says the first one can't be measured,"
and I watch their face do the thing. The confusion isn't theirs. It's mine, structurally, sitting
in the repo as two directories called `paper/` and `paper2/` like they were siblings.

They were never siblings. `paper2` is what happened when `paper1` was tested properly. That's a
parent-child relationship, or more honestly a cause-of-death. Keeping them side by side implied I
was running two research programmes when I was running one programme that changed its mind.

So today: **one paper.** `paper_main/`. 36 pages, builds clean, 0 overfull boxes, 0 undefined refs.

### How the merge actually went

The temptation was to write a "Part I / Part II" thing where the theory gets its own half and the
impossibility result gets the other. I'm glad I didn't. That framing would have required me to
present the coupling capacity results as standing, and they don't stand — I put a red SUPERSEDED box
on `paper/main.tex` weeks ago saying exactly why, and quietly un-saying it inside a merged paper
would have been the most cowardly possible move.

What I did instead: **§2 is the attempt.** Coupling capacity gets stated properly, as Definition 1.
The dimensional bottleneck theorem gets its full proof — data processing inequality plus a
tangent-space count, and I still believe it. P1–P3 are stated. The old figures are Figure 1. And
then §2.3 has a paragraph called *"What this established"* whose first line is:

> Less than it appeared to, and we state the shortfall plainly because the rest of the paper is
> about what follows from it.

Then it says the thing I have to keep saying out loud: for P3, a coupling parameter was **imposed on
the simulator and then recovered** at $r = 1.00$. That's a demonstration that the estimator inverts
the generative model. It is a property of the estimator. It is not evidence for the theory. I
presented it as confirmation at the time and it wasn't.

§2.4 is the pivot: transfer entropy is a functional of the observational distribution, so it's a
$\Phi$ under Definition 4, so Theorem 6 eats it and Proposition 16 closes the directionality escape.
The programme wasn't defeated by a disappointing experiment. It was **aimed one rung too low.**

The nice thing — and I didn't expect this — is that the paper is *better* with §2 in it. The
non-identifiability result read a bit like a drive-by when it opened cold. With the attempt in
front of it, it reads like something I earned by failing at the constructive version first. The
conclusion now has a line I actually like: *"We arrived at this by trying to build the opposite."*

Rewrote the README too. The old one had a table with three predictions all marked **✅ Supported**
and I have been wincing at it for a month. The new one leads with the honest arc — we built the
quantity, it fell over, here's why that's more interesting — and it has a section called "About the

correction written by me than found by a referee.

### What I keep noticing

The theorem survived. That's the part I want to remember from today. $C \le \bar{c}\cdot\min(d_i,d_j)$
is still true — a bandwidth-limited interface can only drive so many independent directions into a
receiver of finite effective dimension, and no amount of wire fixes that. What died wasn't the
mathematics. What died was the assumption that the bound was measuring something the receiver
*cares about*.

An unused channel is trivially consistent with any upper bound on what it could have driven. I wrote
that sentence into §2.3 and then sat with it for a while, because it's the whole thing in one line.
I spent weeks computing a ceiling on a quantity that was sitting at zero for reasons the ceiling had
nothing to say about.

### Still open

Nothing about today was a new result. It was bookkeeping, and honest bookkeeping at that, but
bookkeeping. The three things that would actually move this are unchanged from the paper's §12:

1. **Run the human–AI dissociation study.** The design is fully pre-registered in Appendix C,
   confound and all. Nobody has run it. It's been "designed" since July 31 and designed is not run.
2. **Find a real biological channel-noise instrument.** The method works on the construction I built
   to demonstrate it and fails ($F = 0.2$) on the first system I didn't. Until it survives a
   hyperscanning corpus it's a method with one favourable test case.
3. **Test hysteresis.** Bandwidth sweep twice on the same dyad, ascending from uncoupled and
   descending from established. If the thresholds separate, cross-sectional measurement of coupling
   capacity is ill-posed and that's a much bigger claim than anything currently in the paper.

Also, genuinely: **§2 has never had a hostile read.** It's the newest prose in the manuscript and
I wrote it today. Printing the whole thing and going at it as a referee is still item 2 on the
handoff list, and now there are six more pages of it.

### Small thing

Deleting `paper/` felt worse than it should have. That directory is where this started — the Shannon
analogy, the canteen-napkin four-paper plan, the night `coupling_lab.py` first produced a saturation
curve and I thought I'd found something. I did find something. Just not that.

Anyway. One paper. Pushing to main.
