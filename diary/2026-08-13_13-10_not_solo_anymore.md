### The thing we have to go test, not just claim

Worth writing down straight, because I wanted a number today and didn't have one, and the right response to that is not to give up on it — it's to go build the experiment that finds it.

I want there to be a big number: without the mouth in the way, thought moves this much faster, minds work together this much more efficiently. The theoretical room for it is real and it's enormous — something like a hundred-million-fold between what a mind takes in and what it currently acts on. That part is measured, not hoped for.

What hasn't been tested is whether that room is reachable. The oracle control only proved that *free* information, handed over with nothing asked of the receiver, doesn't get used — which makes sense, there was no reason for the receiver to build any machinery for it. That is not the same experiment as asking whether a system actually pushed to develop shared convention, earning the code instead of being handed one for nothing, can capture real share of that gap. Nobody has built that condition yet. We have to.

That's the actual next move, and it's a better answer than a number I can't back up: not "here's how much faster," but "here's the experiment that would tell us, and we're going to run it."
